CGBB Brand Trust Baseline

1Scope

The Baseline assesses a brand: the organisation behind it and the public record that organisation has built. It applies to companies of any size, industry or market.

It measures how well trust is evidenced. It does not test products, audit accounts or give legal opinions, and a result is not a certification, audit or guarantee of a company’s products, quality, financial standing, compliance or conduct.

2Principles

Evidence, not claims

Every indicator is rated from concrete checks. “Not sure” counts as no evidence.

Same evidence, same result

The rules are fixed and versioned. Nobody adjusts a result by hand.

Risks are not averaged away

A major unresolved risk limits the score, however strong the other sections are.

“Not enough information” is a result

Where the evidence is too thin to judge, the Baseline says so and gives no score.

Public rules

This text, the scoring rules and the change log are public, and anyone can comment.

3Five sections, 15 indicators

Each section asks one question about the brand. Each indicator is rated from the checks listed under it (73 checks in all). Select an indicator to read its checks.

A

Identifiability and consistency

Who are you, can outsiders confirm it, and do the sources match?
A1Verifiable legal entityFact checks · 5 checks

Score = 5 × the share of checks met.

  1. A1.1
    Our website and official channels name the company (legal entity) behind the brand.
  2. A1.2
    We state the country or region where that company is registered.
  3. A1.3
    Anyone can look the company up in a government company registry or other official record.Key check
  4. A1.4
    It is clear which company owns or runs the brand.
  5. A1.5
    We publish a business address or contact details that match that company.
A2Clear management and responsibility informationFact checks · 5 checks

Score = 5 × the share of checks met.

  1. A2.1
    Our management, founders or key people are named publicly.
  2. A2.2
    We have a company profile on LinkedIn or a similar professional platform.
  3. A2.3
    Our key managers, including those leading overseas markets, have public profiles that can be checked.
  4. A2.4
    We publish working contact channels for customers, business partners and media.
  5. A2.5
    In our important overseas markets, people can find who is responsible locally (company, team or contact).
A3Consistent, current information across channelsFact checks · 4 checks

Score = 5 × the share of checks met.

  1. A3.1
    Our name, company, location and main business are the same on our website, the company registry and main platforms.
  2. A3.2
    The language versions of our website agree on the core facts.
  3. A3.3
    Our LinkedIn and similar profiles match our website and registry details.
  4. A3.4
    Our core company information is up to date.
B

Reviews and delivery record

What records have real customers and partners left?
B1Customer feedbackExternal record · 5 checks

Score = the average of the overall performance of the records (1–5) and 5 × the share of checks met. Needs enough records to judge.

  1. B1.1
    Customer ratings, reviews or complaint records about us can be found and traced.
  2. B1.2
    We have a real track record on the main review, marketplace or complaint platforms in our markets.
  3. B1.3
    The number, dates and spread of those reviews can be seen.
  4. B1.4
    The same complaint or negative experience recurs across records.
  5. B1.5
    Complaints and disputes are shown as resolved, closed or being followed up.
B2Partner delivery and cooperation recordExternal record · 5 checks

Score = the average of the overall performance of the records (1–5) and 5 × the share of checks met. Needs enough records to judge.

  1. B2.1
    Our relationships with customers, suppliers or buyers can be confirmed independently.
  2. B2.2
    There are records of repeat orders, multi-year cooperation or long-term projects.
  3. B2.3
    Major clients, projects or partners we name can be confirmed by them or by other sources.
  4. B2.4
    Third-party credit or payment records about us exist.
  5. B2.5
    There are confirmed major delivery disputes, long overdue payments, serious breaches or broken partnerships.
B3Other real external experienceExternal record · 5 checks

Score = the average of the overall performance of the records (1–5) and 5 × the share of checks met. Needs enough records to judge.

  1. B3.1
    Employees, professional users or other stakeholders have left feedback about us that can be traced.
  2. B3.2
    It is clear who left that feedback, when, and in what context.
  3. B3.3
    The platforms where it appears verify purchases or identities.
  4. B3.4
    The same experience or problem recurs across several records.
  5. B3.5
    Main external sources contradict each other in ways that are not explained.
C

Compliance and governance

Are the rules met, and are there material risks?
C1Market access and required licencesFact checks · 5 checks

Score = 5 × the share of checks met.

  1. C1.1
    We hold the licences, registrations or certifications needed to operate in our target markets.Key check
  2. C1.2
    They are valid today.
  3. C1.3
    They can be checked in an official database (regulator, issuing body or similar).
  4. C1.4
    A key licence has expired, been revoked or materially changed in scope.
  5. C1.5
    Expired, revoked or out-of-scope licence information is still being used.
C2Compliance and governance foundationsMaturity level · 8 checks

Score = the maturity level (0–5), limited by the share of checks met.

  1. C2.1
    Someone in the company is responsible for compliance, suited to our size and risks.
  2. C2.2
    We have rules or channels for business conduct, anti-bribery, anti-fraud or whistle-blowing.
  3. C2.3
    We have privacy and data-protection policies, and someone responsible for them.
  4. C2.4
    We can show we own, or have the right to use, our main brands, products and technologies.
  5. C2.5
    Claims about our products' performance, health, safety or environmental benefits are backed by evidence.
  6. C2.6
    We have policies or systems for labour and supply-chain rules.
  7. C2.7
    We screen for sanctions and export controls.
  8. C2.8
    We meet any other compliance requirements that apply to us, and can show it.
C3Legal, regulatory and governance recordRecord scale · six-level scale

Rated on the record scale: the level that matches the organisation’s legal, regulatory and governance record.

  1. 5
    No legal or regulatory problems. We have checked court, regulator and company-registry records and found nothing significant.
  2. 4
    Past problems only, all resolved. Any past cases, fines or findings are closed or fixed, and have not happened again.
  3. 3
    A routine matter is still open. For example a commercial dispute or a regulator's inquiry that we are handling.
  4. 2
    A confirmed problem we are fixing. For example a fine or a finding against us, with corrective action under way.
  5. 1
    Confirmed problems that keep recurring or remain unresolved. The same kind of problem has happened more than once, or has not been fixed.
  6. 0
    Serious violations. For example major penalties, a revoked licence or criminal findings.
D

Transparency and response

When answers are needed, how does the company explain and act?
D1Necessary disclosureMaturity level · 5 checks

Score = the maturity level (0–5), limited by the share of checks met.

  1. D1.1
    Customers, partners, staff and others can easily find the information they need to judge us.
  2. D1.2
    When we make major factual claims, we say where the data comes from or how it was measured.
  3. D1.3
    We make clear what is confirmed fact, what is a forecast and what is opinion.
  4. D1.4
    We update or correct important public information when it changes.
  5. D1.5
    When something major happens, we explain the confirmed facts to the people affected, within legal limits.
D2Complaint and dispute responseMaturity level · 6 checks

Score = the maturity level (0–5), limited by the share of checks met. If no complaint, dispute or major issue arose in the period, the score is limited to 3: a prepared process has not yet been tested.

  1. D2.1
    We have a clear channel for complaints, disputes or major issues.
  2. D2.2
    Someone in the company owns the response, with a basic process.
  3. D2.3
    When cases came up, we responded.
  4. D2.4
    It can be shown when and how we responded.
  5. D2.5
    Our responses state the facts confirmed so far, with evidence where needed.
  6. D2.6
    Our responses set out a fix, remedy or corrective plan, and who is responsible.
D3Remediation and follow-upMaturity level · 5 checks

Score = the maturity level (0–5), limited by the share of checks met. If no complaint, dispute or major issue arose in the period, the score is limited to 3: a prepared process has not yet been tested.

  1. D3.1
    The fixes or measures we announced were actually carried out.
  2. D3.2
    They produced results that can be checked (case closed, compensation paid, recall completed, fix released, policy changed).
  3. D3.3
    We give follow-up updates on matters still in progress.
  4. D3.4
    Sources outside the company confirm those measures or results.
  5. D3.5
    The same problem has happened again since.
E

Responsibility and localisation

What local, long-term record has the company built?
E1Verifiable responsibility practiceMaturity level · 5 checks

Score = the maturity level (0–5), limited by the share of checks met.

  1. E1.1
    We have responsibility (ESG/CSR) practices, goals or projects linked to our business and local operations.
  2. E1.2
    We have acted on these commitments, not only written policies or publicity.
  3. E1.3
    We have kept these actions going over time.
  4. E1.4
    They have produced results or progress records that can be checked.
  5. E1.5
    Third-party partners, certifications or assurance support them.
E2Local hiring and partnershipsMaturity level · 5 checks

Score = the maturity level (0–5), limited by the share of checks met.

  1. E2.1
    We employ people locally in our overseas markets.
  2. E2.2
    We invest in training and developing local staff.
  3. E2.3
    We work with local business partners or suppliers.
  4. E2.4
    This local hiring and cooperation has continued over time.
  5. E2.5
    Records outside the company confirm this.
E3Community and long-term local relationshipsMaturity level · 5 checks

Score = the maturity level (0–5), limited by the share of checks met.

  1. E3.1
    We take part in local community activities, industry bodies or other local events.
  2. E3.2
    We have kept taking part over time, not only in one-off events.
  3. E3.3
    This has built lasting relationships that outsiders can see.
  4. E3.4
    It has produced visible results for our local operations or community relations.
  5. E3.5
    Local partners, industry bodies, community groups or other records confirm it.

Key checks: if a key check is not met, the indicator is limited to 1. Sections A and C each have a key indicator (A1 and C1).

4How an indicator is rated

Each check is answered Yes, No, Not sure or Not applicable. Checks that do not apply are left out. Every indicator then gets a rating from 0 to 5, or ND (not enough information).

External recordB1, B2, B3The average of two things: the overall performance shown by the records (1–5), and 5 × the share of checks met. ND if there are too few records to judge.

5Scoring rules

  • Section rating (0–5). The average of its rated indicators, to the nearest half point. A section needs at least 2 rated indicators, and its key indicator must be rated; otherwise it is ND.
  • Weak points limit a section. If any indicator is rated 0, or the key indicator is rated 0–1, the section is limited to 3.
  • Score (0–100). 20 × the average of the five section ratings.
  • Major risks limit the score. An unresolved major risk, or A1 or C1 rated 0–1, limits the score to 59. Section A or C rated 0–1 limits it to 39.
  • Not enough information. No score is given if section A or C cannot be rated, if fewer than 4 sections are rated, or if under 60% of the items have an answer.
  • Evidence support. Reported with every result. High: at least 80% of items answered and at least 50% confirmed from an independent source. Medium: at least 60% answered. Low: at least 40%.
  • Rounding. All rounding is half up, in whole-number arithmetic, so a result never depends on the computer that calculates it.
0–19Trust foundations lacking
20–39Trust at an early stage
40–59Trust developing
60–79Trust established
80–100Trust well established
