About the book

When Chinese companies sell abroad through agents, trust rests mostly on product quality and delivery. Once they build factories, brands and teams in overseas markets, that is no longer enough. Local governments, partners, customers and staff want to know whether the company, and the people running it, can be relied on.

Trust is a practical guide to building that trust across cultures. It draws on more than 20 years of work in China and on real cases, with company and brand names removed. It covers:

  • the foundations of cross-cultural trust, with a framework that looks at trust through four lenses: institutions, capability, culture and emotion
  • a localisation path built on the “5R” approach: corporate social responsibility, business relationships, human resources, investor relations and government relations
  • the five cultural gaps companies must cross abroad: communication, sales, management, relationship-building and handling disputes
  • how to repair trust in a crisis with governments, customers, investors, suppliers and employees, plus a special section on using LinkedIn

The book follows the author’s earlier handbook on localisation, 《新出海》, and argues that trust is the deciding factor in whether a company becomes a truly local global business. It is the idea the Council for Global Brand Building was founded on.

About the author

Chris Pereira (彭家荣) is Founding Chairman of the Council for Global Brand Building. A Canadian with more than 20 years in China, he first came to the country to study Chinese at Zhengzhou University.

He was previously Senior Director of Global Public Affairs at Huawei. He holds an MBA from HKUST, teaches at Xi’an Jiaotong-Liverpool University’s International Business School, and was named by Forbes China as one of the top 30 pioneers in Chinese brand globalization.
