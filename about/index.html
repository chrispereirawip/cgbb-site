About CGBB

A non-profit council that sets the standards for trust in business.

The Council for Global Brand Building (CGBB, 品信會) was founded in Hong Kong by a consortium of companies, institutions and media groups, to establish the rules for building trust in global business. It was announced in Geneva in September 2026, during the WTO Public Forum.

Why trust, and why now

Geopolitics, AI-generated content and automated agents make it harder to know who you are really dealing with. Buyers increasingly ask AI before they ask a person. Trust that can be checked saves everyone time; trust that is only claimed does not travel. CGBB calls the distance between the two the trust gap:

Capability is not perception.

Strong products, technology and operations do not automatically shape how a company is seen.

Awareness is not trust.

Being known is the start of the work. Being trusted, and being able to show it, is the goal.

Entering a market is not belonging to it.

Selling into a market and becoming a trusted part of it call for different capabilities.

How CGBB keeps its work impartial

A body that sets standards for trust has to be checkable itself. These are the rules CGBB works to.

  1. One set of rules for everyone

    Every organisation, including CGBB’s founding institutions, applies in the same way and waits for Secretariat approval before its Website Trust Credential is issued.

  2. A person reviews every application

    No application is approved automatically, whether it comes through the website, a partner or an AI agent.

  3. Results follow published rules

    Baseline results follow the fixed rules in the public standard. The same evidence always gives the same result.

  4. Fees do not buy results

    Basic Membership is free. Advanced Membership, when it opens, will pay for the work of a Trust Review, not for its outcome. A brand that does not meet the standard is not listed as Verified Trusted.

  5. Membership is not endorsement

    The Member level confirms who an organisation is and that it controls its website. It is not a certification, rating or endorsement.

  6. Every badge can be checked

    A genuine badge links to a live record in the registry. When a membership ends, the record changes at once, and CGBB may act against misuse of its marks.

  7. The standard is open to comment

    Anyone can comment on the draft standard. Changes are versioned and listed in a public change log.

  8. Private data stays private

    Self-test answers and reports are seen only by the member and the Secretariat. The public record shows the organisation, its website, its level and its status.
